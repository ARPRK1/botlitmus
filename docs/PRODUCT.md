# BotLitmus — product

## One sentence

A public league table of whether Indian help centres and support bots tell the same story as the rest of the site.

## Why this exists

Rana Appannagari already knows support operations. Cold-emailing Chargebee for a ₹2,500 teardown failed because he has no warm network. This product is the same teardown, indexed. Distribution is the report URL.

Strangers find `/report/nykaa`. The brand finds itself. The brand pays to retest.

## Offer ladder

| Thing | Price | When |
|---|---|---|
| Public report (this site) | Free | Now |
| Full PDF, same evidence plus fix order | ₹999 | After 15+ public reports, Razorpay |
| Monthly retest of one domain | ₹4,999 | After first paid PDF |
| Private probe of a logged-in bot | custom | Later. Not in v1. |

Do not add a fourth product.

## What v1 does

1. League table of published reports.
2. One URL per brand: `/report/{slug}`.
3. Paste-a-URL first pass (`/scan`) that crawls public pages and runs the heuristic rubric.
4. Method page so a journalist or founder can attack the score.

## What v1 does not do

- Does not log into a chatbot and chat with it.
- Does not claim “AI detected hallucinations” without a stored transcript.
- Does not sell trading signals, YouTube, or a new SaaS.
- Does not scrape private data, Practo, or Google Maps.

## Buyer

Head of CX / founder of an Indian D2C or SaaS brand whose bot or help centre just appeared on a public list.

## Voice

Short sentences. Evidence first. No “unlock”, “leverage”, “seamless”, “game-changer”. No em dashes. Never invent a metric, a client, or a quote.

## Name

**BotLitmus**. Tagline: “Does this help centre tell the truth?”
